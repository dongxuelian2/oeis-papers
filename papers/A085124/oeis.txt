COMMENTS
The stated conjecture holds: a(n)>0 if and only if n is 7-smooth and 10 does not divide n. An elementary proof constructs a zero-free suffix divisible by the full 2- or 5-power in n, prepends the digits supplying a positive product divisible by n, then repeats the block to obtain divisibility by the part coprime to 10.

FORMULA
For n=2^a*3^b*5^c*7^d with min(a,c)=0, let e=(a,b,c,d). Use a finite automaton with states (r,f), 0<=r<n, 0<=f_p<=e_p, p in {2,3,5,7}. Reading t in {1,...,9} sends (r,f) to ((10*r+t) mod n,(min(e_p,f_p+valuation(t,p)))_p). From an extra nonaccepting initial vertex use the outgoing edges of (0,0), and accept at (0,e). Then a(n) is the shortest lexicographically first accepted word, interpreted as an integer. Its length is at most n*(a+1)*(b+1)*(c+1)*(d+1). For all other n, a(n)=0.
