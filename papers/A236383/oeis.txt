A236383 update draft (not submitted), 2026-10-26

The existence conjecture is true for every positive n. Let D be the
number of decimal digits of 4(n+1)^2, and choose the least d>=D with
d odd and 5d+2n=0 (mod 11); then D<=d<=D+21. Put C=(10^d+1)/11,
k=n+5C, x=5(2n+5C)/11, and y=x+n^2. The binomial expansion gives
C=d (mod11), so x is integral. Both x and y have d digits, and
k^2=10^d*x+y. Thus a(n)<=n+5C.

An exact finite minimum formula follows by considering all lengths
1..d and all roots of r^2=n^2 modulo 10^length+1 in the interval
ceil(sqrt((10^length+1)*10^(length-1)+n^2))
through floor(sqrt((10^length+1)*(10^length-1-n^2)+n^2)).
All roots are explicitly listed at odd prime powers and combined
by CRT. The first nonempty length gives the minimum; no larger
length can give a smaller k.

The complete published 245-term b-file is reproduced and a new
300-term table is supplied. An independent exhaustive scan of
k=1..1000000 agrees on all 659 attained indices and their minima.

Proof and verifier: papers/A236383/main.tex and verify.py in the
oeis-papers repository. No elementary expression without finite
minimization is asserted.
