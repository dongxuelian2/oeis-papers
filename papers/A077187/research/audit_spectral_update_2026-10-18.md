## 1. Period and coset audit

Write `r=uv`, `u=2^a*5^c`, `(v,10)=1`, and

```

T=m*ell=lcm(r,h), zeta=10^m mod p.
```

Here `ord_1(10)=1`. Since `v|r` and `v|ell`, one has `v|g`. The first
layer of the block `T+1,...,2T` is stable: `T>=max(a,c)` suffices, and
follows from `T>=r`. Every stable exponent
`e_d=9*d*10^(d-1)` is divisible by `u`.

The multiplier `A_d=10^e_d` has period `ell`: its exponents agree modulo
`u` because both are zero there, and agree modulo `v` because `v|ell`
and `10^ell=1 mod v`.

For the product over `ell` layers, the extra condition

```
v divides (10^ell-1)/9
```

is essential and is correctly supplied. At primes other than 3 it follows
by cancelling 9; at 3, LTE gives
`v_3((10^ell-1)/9)=v_3(ell)>=v_3(v)`. The telescoping exponent identity
in the note is correct. Its right side reduces modulo `v` to
`ell*10^(d-1)=0`. The `u`-divisibility is termwise. Thus a short block's
product is one, and its cumulative phase `P` is periodic with period
`ell`, including when `v` is divisible by a high power of 3.

For a fixed `j=1,...,ell`, the actual values
`10^(T+j+t*ell)`, `0<=t<m`, form a coset of the subgroup generated by
`10^ell`, whose order is exactly `r/gcd(r,ell)=m`. They are distinct,
and their m-th powers all equal `zeta^j`. Because `m|p-1`, they comprise
all roots of `X^m-zeta^j` and none is repeated. This justifies summing
over the complete root coset; it is not an assumption about distribution.

The stable multiplier is

```
A_j=zeta^((g/v)*gamma*9*j*10^(j-1)), gamma=u^(-1) mod v.
```

Indeed `10^u=zeta^(g/v)`, and division of the stable exponent by `u`
modulo `v` gives the displayed exponent. Every actual `(a,c)` supplies
one of the finite coefficient phases. For `v=7`, `g=7` or 14; for
`v=11`, `g=11` or 22. Enumerating all nonzero residues `gamma` covers
every actual phase, even when some enumerated phases are redundant for

## 2. Singular terms, polynomial coordinates, and denominators

## 3. Degenerate elimination and norm logic

For quadratic forms `F=a+b*m+c*m^2`, `G=d+e*m+f*m^2`, set
`A=c*d-a*f`, `B=c*e-b*f`, `C=b*d-a*e`. A common finite root gives

```
A+B*m=c*G-f*F=0,
C-B*m^2=b*G-e*F=0,
```

and hence `A^2-B*C=0`. This implication uses no division by a leading
coefficient and remains valid after any degree drop modulo `p`.

For `v=11`, both quadratic coefficients vanish identically. The script
correctly changes to the linear determinant `U_1*V_0-U_0*V_1`; retaining
the padded quadratic expression would have given zero identically and
no exclusion. If two linear forms have a common root, their determinant
vanishes even if one or both slopes vanish modulo `p`. Constant or zero
polynomial degeneracies therefore do not invalidate necessity.

At the actual phase, evaluation `zeta -> 10^m` sends the common-root
obstruction to zero. Since this image has exact order `g`, the scaled
obstruction and `Phi_g` share a root modulo `p`; their resultant/norm
is divisible by `p`. The proof needs only this necessary implication,
not the converse. All 12 quadratic norms and all 20 linear norms were
recomputed and were nonzero.

The involutive degree-drop proof is sound: `10^2=1 mod v` implies
`A_(-j)=A_j^(-1)` and `P_(ell+1-j)=P_j`. Therefore `alpha` and `beta`
are odd under `j -> ell-j`, whereas the quadratic coefficient
`c/(c-1)^2`, extended by `-1/12` at 1, is even under inversion. Fixed
points contribute zero. This is an exact identity in the cyclotomic
field, including `v=3,9,33,99`; it is not inferred from sample primes.

## 4. Coverage of all nonnegative a,c

a huge cyclotomic integer. Although the v=7 note says the script
"verifies" the substitution identity, the script uses it; the algebraic
identity above is its mathematical justification.

next `c`. All 3943 admissible pairs are covered.

Repeated division by `gcd(h,r)` removes exactly factors supported on
primes dividing the index. A prime of exact order `r` cannot divide

verified final residual 1 excludes all possible target primes, without

Finally, full-layer maps repeat after `T`, and their linear part over

### Odd branch: an exact nonvanishing invariant

For `g=11`, choose the representative `A` in `1,...,10` of
`10*gamma mod 11`, and put `z=zeta^A`, `d=11-2*A`. Then

```

V = -81/200*(1+z) - (11*d/20)*m*(1-z).
```

All ten instances agree exactly with the delivered symbolic coordinates.
Their linear terms cancel in

```

```

Here `z` has exact order 11, so `z!=-1`. The only possible extra prime
from `909=9*101` is 101, and `ord_101(10)=4`, not a multiple of 11.

simultaneous zero drift is impossible. This is an algebraic,
certificate-free proof for

The finite Fourier sums behind the closed form are also consistent:

over the 11th roots, with the root 1 included through the stated extension.
