OEIS A340138 update draft (not submitted)

NAME CORRECTION
a(1)=0; a(n+1) is the least unused nonnegative integer containing
the decimal digital root of a(n) among its digits.
(This immediate-predecessor indexing agrees with the entry's program
and all its displayed terms; the existing a(n-1) reference does not.)

COMMENTS
The sequence is a permutation of the nonnegative integers. The same
construction gives a permutation in every integer base b>=2.

PROOF
All steps exist because infinitely many numbers contain a specified
digit. Some positive digital root r occurs infinitely often. Every
number M containing digit r must then appear: otherwise infinitely
many terms immediately after root-r predecessors would be distinct
unused candidates <=M, an impossibility. For each other digital root
s, infinitely many numbers have digit r and root s: take decimal
words r followed by L>=1 zeros and digit d, with 0<=d<=8 and
r+d == s mod 9. All such numbers appear, so every root occurs
infinitely often. Every positive integer contains a nonzero digit s,
and is forced to appear by the first argument. Zero is the initial
term, and repetitions are excluded by the rule.
